Running reviews this year? Start with the scale.

The review system I was proudest of didn't survive me leaving. Here's what that taught me about getting performance reviews right.

VVidah Claire Kazibwe, CEO, Phela·Tuesday 17 November 2026·5 min read

A few years ago, I built a performance review system I was genuinely proud of. Career tracks for every role. Competencies with clear levels. Regular check-ins that built up to a proper year-end review, calibrated across teams. Honestly, it was robust. I'd spent months on it, and on paper it was the kind of thing you'd present at a conference.

Then I left.

A few months later I caught up with someone from the team and asked how reviews were going. "Oh, we simplified it," she said. They now had one form with three questions: what should you start doing, what should you stop doing, and what should you keep doing. The career tracks? Out of the window.

I'll be honest… my first reaction was a small, private ouch. All that work. But the longer I sat with it, the more I realised they hadn't thrown away a good system. They had chosen one that fit where they were. And mine, at that moment, didn't.

That conversation changed how I think about performance reviews. Whether you're in HR, in Finance, or the CEO running reviews yourself, here's what I learned.

1. Reviews sit on a scale

Picture a line from 1 to 10. At 1 is the simplest review that still works: a few questions, a real conversation, and a note of what was agreed. At 10 you have competencies, skills, goals, career levels and calibration across teams.

Neither end is "better". A 10 in a business that needs a 2 turns into paperwork nobody finishes. A 2 in a business that needs a 9 leaves good people without a path to grow. Getting reviews right isn't about picking the best method. It's about knowing where your company sits on that scale right now, and building for that.

2. The scale moves

This is the part nobody warns you about. A company can sit at a 9 today and at a 2 six months from now. New leaders arrive. A big hiring push brings in lots of new people. A restructure changes who does what. Funding changes what everyone has time for.

My system was built for a moment. The moment passed, the scale moved, and the team did the sensible thing. So "where do we sit?" isn't a question you answer once. It's "where do we sit now?", asked again and again.

3. What to watch so you know it's moving

You don't need a survey every month. Three things tell you most of what you need to know.

The make-up of your roles. Are most people in a few similar roles, or spread across many specialist ones? Field teams, operations, specialists, managers… the more varied the roles, the more structure your reviews can carry and the more they need.

Your people, and what they can take in. Think about tenure and experience, how comfortable people are with forms and written feedback, and the language and devices they use every day. A review that works for a finance team on laptops may not land for a field team on phones.

Your leaders. Are your managers ready to run reviews, and do they have the time? What do they want reviews to do for them? And how do they want to be seen: as coaches, as assessors, as people who get it done without fuss? Reviews ask a lot of managers, and they only stick when managers can carry them.

When any of these shifts, your place on the scale has probably shifted too.

4. One review can be simple and complex at the same time

This is the lesson I wish I'd learned first. A single review cycle doesn't have to be one size.

Your field team might answer three questions on their phone each month. Your managers might be rated against competencies, goals and their level, then calibrated with their peers. Same cycle, same timeline, two very different experiences, each one right for the person in it.

Looking back, my mistake wasn't building a 10. It was giving everyone the same 10.

So what do you do with this?

  • Find your number. Look at your roles, your people and your leaders, and be honest about where you sit today.
  • Design for that number, not for the number you'd like to be.
  • Let different groups sit at different points on the scale inside the same cycle.
  • Put a date in the diary six months from now, and check again.
