Knowledge hub / Running HR without HR

No HR yet? Choose an HR system that works.

You don't have an HR person yet, and you're wondering whether an HR system is worth it for a business your size. We think it is, and it might be the reason you don't need to hire HR for a while. But not every system takes work off your plate. Here's what to look for.

CClaire, Founder, JaliHR·Tuesday 3 November 2026·9 min read

For over ten years, we've worked with companies running without an HR team, and there's a belief we hear a lot. HR systems are for companies with an HR department. You buy one when you hire your HR person, they pick it, they run it. Until then, it's spreadsheets, shared folders and a lot of goodwill. Honestly, with the HR systems we've all had to choose from, we can see the logic.

But with all the new, modern, AI-powered HR tech, we see it the other way round. A system is most useful before you have HR, because there's nobody whose full-time job it is to keep things in order. And with systems like ours that are heavily automated, a team your size gets an incredible amount of HR lift, enough to push that "time to hire HR" date a lot further out.

But here's the catch, and we've seen it more times than we'd like. A system set up badly is a system that gives you more work, not less. Logins nobody uses. Information typed in twice, once in the system and once in the spreadsheet you kept "just in case". A payroll that still needs a calculator beside it. Six months later, you're back to the shared folder… plus a subscription.

So if you're a team without an HR person, here are six things a good HR system should get done for you, what to look out for in each, and what it should cost. Grab a drink, settle in and read on.

What a good HR system should do for you

1. Hold your employee records, end to end

Your system should collect employee records, hold them, and actually put them to work. Collect means people fill in their own details and upload their own documents, once, at the start. Hold means everything lives in one place you can find in seconds, not across three inboxes and a WhatsApp chat. Put to work means those records feed everything else: payroll, contracts, leave, filing.

One thing to look out for that most people miss: every employee should have their own account, and they should keep access to it even after they leave. Their access to the company switches off, but they can still log in and download their payslips, certificates, contract, whatever they need. If you've ever had a former employee call six months later asking for a payslip for a loan application… you know exactly why this matters.

This alone takes the filing, the record keeping and the "can you send me my…" requests off your desk, and frees your time for the things you actually need to do.

2. Run contracts and the everyday HR work for you

Contracts first. Your system should create them, hold them alongside every other document, track them, record approvals and, most of all, remind you before anything changes: probation ending, a contract about to expire, a renewal due. That replaces the Excel sheet you check "every now and then", the scramble to catch up on contracts at audit time, and, honestly, the awkward moment you notice an employee looking a little annoyed because their contract expired and they don't know how to ask whether they're getting a renewal.

The same goes for the rest of the everyday HR work. Leave should be requested, approved and tracked without you counting days. Disciplinary steps should be recorded as they happen, so the paper trail exists before you need it. And you should be able to see the basics at a glance: who's on leave, who's on probation, whose contract ends this quarter. Benefits belong here too.

3. Payroll: you're buying time back, not a system

This is actually one of those where you don't need a system just for payroll. A spreadsheet with 20 to 30 employees is fine. Honestly. But it's going to take you anywhere from an entire afternoon to five days to get payroll prepped every month, and don't even get me started on the manual payslips.

So what you're really looking for isn't a payroll system. It's time back. Payroll tools like JaliHR, Gusto or Deel are built to remove the work and have your payroll ready in under 10 minutes: payroll prepared, approvals queued, statutory deductions worked out and ready to file (or filed for you), payment files created. You focus on the paying.

When you look at a system, ask one question: how long will my payroll take each month once this is set up? If you don't have an HR person and the answer is still "a day", keep looking.

4. Performance reviews: know where your money is working

It's review season, and this is the one teams without HR tend to skip, or rush, or move to "next quarter". Understandable, there's always something more urgent. But we forget one thing: the salary you pay is in direct relation to the output you're given. If you're paying people and you have no way to track whether that work is giving you a return… you're throwing money in a pit and lighting it on fire.

But you're right, you don't need something so complicated nobody can run it. You want a system that maps each role to what good looks like, sets clear benchmarks, gives managers an easy review to fill in, and then tells you where your money is working and where it's being set on fire. You want something that takes 30 minutes to set up and 5 minutes for each manager to submit a review. If it's more complicated than that, you don't need it yet.

5. Talks to finance, automatically

If your system cannot send reports to your finance team or your finance system automatically at this stage… throw it away. In our experience, HR and finance reconciliation can take up to 10 hours a month: salary advances, benefits, payroll, who was hired, who left, commissions. It's a whole mess to pull together, and it's the biggest source of friction between HR work and finance work.

So if you're getting a system, let it get this right. Ideally it connects straight to your accounting software. If it can't, it had better be able to email a report or a journal to your finance person on a schedule, every month, without anyone asking.

6. Put at least half the work on autopilot

Honestly, in the age of AI, if at least 50% of your HR work can't run on autopilot in the system… don't get it. Reminders should send themselves. Approvals should route themselves. Documents should file themselves. Payroll should arrive prepared. And yes, you should be able to connect your AI and simply ask it to do the HR task for you.

A quick test: ask the provider to show you everything that happens without anyone clicking a button. If that list is short, so is the time you'll get back.

What it should cost

A system that does all of the above shouldn't cost more than $4 per employee per month, up to about 50 employees. As your team grows past that, negotiate it down: the bigger your team, the further below $4 it should go.

For 50 employees, that's $200 a month. A skilled HR person, even in the less expensive markets, starts at around $500 a month. So you're getting the work done for about 60% less, while you decide when the right time to hire is.

And so…

Bottom line: a good system at a business your size, set up and run right, can push back the day you need to hire an HR person by years. The key words are "set up right". A system you have to set up alone, on top of everything else, is exactly the kind that ends up giving you more work.
